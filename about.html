THE PROBLEM

Online scams are becoming harder to recognize

Fraudulent messages increasingly imitate banks, delivery companies, government agencies, employers and trusted brands. They often pressure users to act before they have time to verify the request.

  • !
    Urgency and pressure

    Scammers use deadlines, threats and limited-time warnings to prevent careful thinking.

  • !
    Trusted-name impersonation

    Fake messages may appear to come from banks, police, support teams or familiar companies.

  • !
    Payment and data requests

    Users may be asked to share OTPs, passwords, card details or make immediate payments.

IMPORTANT LIMITATIONS

TrustLens supports decisions, but does not replace verification

The current detection engine uses browser-based rules and pattern matching. It can identify common warning signals, but it cannot guarantee that content is completely safe or fraudulent.

  • A low-risk result does not guarantee that a message or website is safe.
  • New or sophisticated scams may not match existing detection rules.
  • OCR results can contain errors when screenshots are unclear or text is too small.
  • Users should verify suspicious requests through official websites, apps or phone numbers.
